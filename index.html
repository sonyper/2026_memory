What your results mean

What researchers would expect to find

Misinformation effect: Across a group, participants who read misleading post-event information are expected, on average, to make more errors consistent with that misinformation than participants who read accurate information. This reflects the reconstructive nature of memory: remembering is not a perfect replay, and later information can become blended with the original event.

Source-monitoring errors: A participant may remember a detail but confuse where it came from— the video or the later social media post. This can make a suggested detail feel like something they actually witnessed.

Feedback and confidence: Strongly confirming feedback may increase confidence even when an answer is incorrect, because confidence is influenced by later social feedback as well as by the quality of the original memory. Confidence and accuracy therefore do not always move together.

These are predicted group-level patterns, not a diagnosis or a pass/fail standard for an individual participant.

Why your results might differ from the expected pattern

Individual results can vary because of attention while watching the video, how clearly a detail was encoded, prior expectations, the similarity of the vehicles, the delay between viewing and recall, and whether the participant correctly remembers the source of a detail. Random assignment also means one person's result may not show the average effect. A single participant's score cannot establish whether an experimental condition caused an effect.

Full debriefing

This study examines how inaccurate post-event information can influence eyewitness reports (the misinformation effect) and how affirming feedback can influence confidence. The experiment randomly assigned participants to read either an accurate or misleading social media-style post and to receive either strongly confirming or neutral feedback. Feedback was assigned independently of answer accuracy and was not an evaluation of your actual performance.

The post may have included an incorrect detail about the road sign and/or which vehicle approached from each side. The two traffic clips show the same staged collision scenario with different road signs (STOP or YIELD). No identifying information is requested by this page.
